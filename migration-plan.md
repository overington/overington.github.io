## Checklist

- [ ] **1. Identify the hosting setup.** Confirm the domain's document root in cPanel, the SSH account and port, whether other sites share the document root, and which databases and hosting features belong to the site. Check available disk space and backup options before creating archives. Do not assume `public_html` is the right destination.
- [ ] **2. Contain and assess the compromise.** Contact Serversaurus support about the compromise and hosting-specific backup and cleanup guidance. From a trusted device, review and rotate compromised account credentials and access as appropriate; consider the risk of leaving the compromised site online. Coordinate timing so the backup and future access are not disrupted.
- [ ] **3. Back up the existing site.** Consider a cPanel account backup if available and feasible within the account quota; otherwise consider streaming the files directly to local storage and exporting the database separately. Ensure the site's files, hidden files, database, and relevant configuration are covered. An account backup may include other sites, mail and sensitive data. Store temporary archives outside publicly served directories, with restrictive permissions. Note when the snapshot was taken; changes after that point will not be included.
- [ ] **4. Download and verify the backup locally.** Keep it outside this repository, preferably on encrypted storage. Where a server-side archive is used, compare its checksum with the local copy. For streamed backups, check transfer errors and compare against the source using an appropriate method. Check archive readability and database export completeness, and retain an additional secure copy. Treat backups as sensitive and potentially infected; do not execute or publish their contents. Remove temporary server copies only after verification.
- [ ] **5. Prepare the Zola site.** Build and inspect it locally; check URLs, assets, redirects and any WordPress content that needs preserving. Confirm the production domain and document root. Identify hosting-managed files, certificate-validation paths, other sites or services that must not be deleted.
- [ ] **6. Preview the deployment.** Review `make deploy-preview` carefully, including its proposed deletions. The Makefile uses `rsync --delete`: stop if any required or unrelated files would be removed, and adjust the approach before deployment. Never target a shared or unconfirmed directory.
- [ ] **7. Deploy and verify.** Only after the backup is verified and the exact deployment and cleanup approach is agreed, replace the site. Check HTTPS, key pages, assets, redirects and hosting functions. Do not restore compromised PHP or other infected files to the live site.
- [ ] **8. Follow up.** Complete credential rotation and access review, verify monitoring and backups, and document a tested recovery approach with the host. A static site does not by itself remove compromise elsewhere in the hosting account.
