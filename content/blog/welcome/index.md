<!-- Why do I want to write a blog -->
Writing, for me, is a non-linear process. This is half manifesto, and half explanation for how my mind works (in non-linear ways). I want to get my ideas out of my head and onto the page to help me make connections between them, clarify what I intend to say, and then repeating this process into a better understanding, or perhaps even a new idea altogether.

<!-- Saying it first -->
Often I'll have a number of disparate concepts bouncing around in my head, and a few connecting ideas how they might string together to form the basis of an understanding; so I'll start writing my stream-of-consciousness, and capture them on the page (sometimes starting with pen and paper). This places my ideas as words physically next to one another, and helps me to see the connections between them, or perhaps the lack thereof.

<!-- Clarifying what I mean -->
Writing is also about finding a way to communicate my ideas more clearly. Further reading passes over my initial writing, I might notice that sometimes my initial thoughts don't make sense, are duplicates of one another, or are a bit bare, and need to be fleshed out, and so I will add, rewrite, reorder, swap and remove ideas - both for clarity and definition. Through editing, as well, my own understanding of a subject is also updated, which can also lead to new insights.

<!-- Communicate my ideas more clearly: -->
<!-- Both of these processes are important, and they take a bit of time to process, -->
So it's not just about *having the thought*, it's also taking the next steps of writing them down, editing and figuring out if it's right or wrong, and then understanding why, and then transforming them into a new form., and then being able to clearly articulate these thoughts.


# Blog post formats (to begin with):

<!-- 
- Structure
-->
Sometimes, I get a bit lost in the process of writing, so I thought it would also be good to set some initial boundaries. This is also so that I'm not approaching the page entirely blank, and I have a format to get started with. This is subject to change in the future, of course, after I use it and see what works and what doesn't.
I want to have different "categories" of blog posts:

- **Creative**: where the end output would have something to show - maybe visual, maybe through code, and the aim will be to uncover something through writing and experimentation.
- **Notes**: where I am thinking out loud about a concept or idea that is knocking around my head.
- **Exploratory**: where I try out a new idea - kind of like brainstorming.
- **Tutorial**: where I walk through something in a methodical step-by-step process, exploring how to do it, what specific parts do, and what their effects are. Maybe this will be turned into its own section on here, but to begin with - here.
- **Paper**: taking a look at a research paper or blog post and walk through it and give my understanding about it. This is probably for my own understanding and notes, so I might only focus on the parts of it that are relevant to me. Again - this might migrate to its own section, or stay here.


# On using AI

In my work, to varying degrees, I use chat models and coding agents to get things done.

As this blog is a place for me to do some thinking out loud, I would primarily be cheating myself if I were to use generative models to write everything for me. However, saying that, I would also not be comfortable calling myself a Luddite, completely rejecting the use of generative AI as a tool. So herein lies the subtle discriminating process that I am experimenting with. I don't have a fully formed idea about how this will work, but I DO have some ideas and guiding principles to begin with:

1. First and foremost: the ideas, structure and concrete content that I write here are my own, and I am responsible for them. I will not use generative AI to come up with new ideas here, or to complete or flesh out the body of writing in a post. I will write my thoughts down first, and then do some initial editing myself.
2. I know that I can get side-tracked with writing, and go on tangents and deep-dives - especially when translating my thoughts directly from my brain. In my opinion, on occasion this (tangent-driven) style of writing might be worthy, and other times just self-indulgent and wouldn't serve my purpose. And to get an opinion about that difference, I might use an LLM to help me see this.
3. Having an LLM pass over for readability can sometimes be useful.

So to sum up - there are three parts of writing: *thoughts and substance*, *structure*, and *style*. I feel more comfortable about using an LLM to help in terms of style, middlingly in terms of structure, and not so much on initial thoughts and substance. So I think the important parts are:

1. I will not use generative AI to "come up" with new ideas
2. I will not use generative AI to write the body or structure of the post
3. I might use generative AI to help with grammar, spelling, flow, and readability, but I wouldn't use its raw output - I would be the one to decide if its proposed changes are what I intend, i.e. I won't blindly copy a model's output.

# On corrections, updates and generally getting things wrong.

I am human, I am fallible. I will get things wrong, make incorrect assumptions and conclusions. For this reason, if there are any updates to a post, I will add an update tag or something, and note where and what has been corrected. If the basis of the written text becomes null and void - I might find a new way of organising my posts, and create something like an "archived" category, noting at the top of the post why it has been archived, and pointing out what was incorrect. If they are small corrections, these will be noted with update metadata, along with changes at the top.
